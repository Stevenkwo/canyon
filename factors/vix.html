What it measures#

VIX is the Cboe Volatility Index: the 30-day implied volatility of S&P 500 index options, quoted as an annualized percentage. A reading of 30 means the options market prices a one-month move in the index of roughly ±9%.

Since 1990 the median close is 17.6, and 8% of days have closed at 30 or above. Those days cluster in a handful of spells: 2008 to 2009, 2020, and short bursts like April 2025.

Above 30 the account treats the tape as an alarm: no new judgment outside the registered triggers, and the R1 dip leg stays off (it needs the previous close below 30).

The Oct 27 close is 17.5, next to the long-run median of 17.6, and the 57th percentile of the past year. This year ran from 14.2 on Sep 22 to 31.1 on Mar 27. The only closes at 30 or above in 2026 were Mar 27 and Mar 30. Nothing is flashing.

How the rules read it#

Level Rule What happens
30 or above Alarm The account executes only its registered triggers and adds no new judgment that day
Previous close below 30 R1 FTNT dip leg The leg may fire on a 2% lower open; with the previous close at 30 or above it stays off
Level, term structure, percentile Risk check VIX is the sentiment corner of a three-way check with the top-warning list and rates; the weekly review reports its percentile

The 30 line is a state switch, not a forecast. It does not say the market will fall; it says the account stops improvising. The three spells at 30 or above since the start of 2025:

Disclosure

How this reading is usedNo position is held on this reading. It gates the R1 FTNT dip leg, which needs the previous close below 30, and sets the account's alarm state at 30 or above.

Readings are computed from public prices with the stated definition. They describe the past and say nothing certain about the future. Notes on this site express the author's opinions.
