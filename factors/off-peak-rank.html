What it measures#

The off-peak spread compares the last 42 trading days of Shaanxi Coal (601225, total return) with the Nasdaq-100 over the same days:

spread = coal's 42-day return − β × QQQ's 42-day return, with β estimated over the last 90 days

The rank is the spread's percentile within its own trailing two years (504 trading days). A high rank means coal has run well ahead of anything the Nasdaq-100 explains; a low rank means it has lagged. In practice β sits close to zero (median +0.03), so the rank is almost exactly coal's own 42-day momentum rank. The rules that read it are on the CF strategy page.

How the rules read it#

Level Rule What happens
90 or above CF band rule, in force Coal target falls to 90% of its anchor
85, after a dip below 50 CF swap rule, on paper 20 points of a 50/50 sleeve move to the Nasdaq-100 at the next close
Below 50 CF swap rule, on paper Back to 50/50 at the next close; the swap re-arms
10 or below CF band rule, in force Coal target rises to 110% of its anchor

A reading of about 61 sits in the band where nothing fires. The October peak of 86.9 crossed the swap line but stayed under the band rule's 90, so the coal target never left 100% of its anchor.

Two versions of the reading#

The line is the research version of the rank, on total-return prices with a 504-day window, the same series the CF strategy page uses. It is cached through Oct 23. Later days are recomputed from raw closes with the same definition and drawn dashed; against the cached series over the past year they differ by a median of about 3 points, so read them as a trend, not to the decimal. The official script, which works on additively adjusted prices and a shorter window until mid-December, read 84.5 at the Oct 12 peak and 50.4 on Oct 23.

Disclosure

How this reading is usedThe rank is computed from public prices. It feeds the CF band rule on the account's Shaanxi Coal holding and the CF swap rule tracked on paper. A reading on its own changes nothing; only a rule firing does.
