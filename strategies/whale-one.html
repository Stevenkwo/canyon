Whale One

TSM by night, AAPL by day, cash on the nights after QQQ closes below its 200-day average, and a TSLA intraday momentum band on top. Three changes to R1, tested on paper.

The rule in one paragraph#

Whale One is R1 with three changes. The overnight stock is TSM instead of NVDA: bought at the closing auction, sold at the next opening auction. The night leg has a gate: if QQQ closed below its 200-day average the day before, no TSM is bought that night and the core sits in cash until the open. And a third intraday leg is added: 50% of NAV in TSLA whenever its price is above a noise band, checked every half hour and always flat by the close. Everything else is R1 as registered: AAPL from open to close, the FTNT gap-down overlay, XOM at 20% under a monthly 200-day gate, USO at 20% for 21 days after OVX falls back through 50.

Rules#

Night · TSM Every trading day, unless the night gate is on Buy at the closing auction, sell at the next opening auction Core
Night gate QQQ closed below its 200-day average of closes on the trading day before the order day No night order; the core is in cash until the next open Core in cash
Day · AAPL Every trading day Buy at the opening auction, sell at the closing auction Core
TSLA noise band At 10:00, 10:30 … 15:30 ET: TSLA above max(today's open, yesterday's close) × (1 + σ), where σ is the average absolute move from the open at that clock time over the previous 14 days Hold while a check finds the price above the band, sell when a check finds it at or below, always sell at the close 50% of NAV, intraday only
FTNT overlay As in R1: pre-market indication at or below 98% of the prior close; not an earnings day or the day after; prior VIX close below 30 Buy at the open, sell at the close 50% of NAV, intraday only
XOM As in R1: first trading day of the month, XOM closes above its 200-day average Hold until the next monthly check 20% of NAV, or nothing
USO As in R1: OVX closes below 50 after closing at or above 50 the day before Buy at that close, sell 21 trading days later 20% of NAV
Core 1 − XOM weight − USO weight 100%, 80% or 60% of NAV

The backtest charges 1.2 basis points per side on every leg. The rotation fills at auction prices. The band leg fills at the close of the five-minute bar that gives the signal.

Each change does a different job.

  • TSM for NVDA leaves the return about where it was and makes the falls shallower. The two night legs move together (daily correlation 0.65) and earned about the same over these eight years; TSM is the less volatile of the two.
  • The night gate was on for 406 of 2,012 nights, 20%. On those nights TSM averaged −0.3 basis points (t = −0.03), so the gate gives up no return and removes a fifth of the nights from the book. On the other nights TSM averaged +22.1 basis points (t = 5.5).
  • The TSLA band adds about 20 points of annual return and makes the deepest fall 1.5 points deeper. It held a position on 619 days, 31%. On those days it made +28.9 basis points on average (t = 3.8) and was up on 46% of them: many small losses and fewer, larger gains. Its daily correlation with the night leg is zero, which is why the Sharpe ratio rises. It is extra exposure on top of the day book, so it adds to a fall on the days it loses.

The fifth row is the same structure with NVDA left in place. It returns eight points more a year and its deepest fall is eleven points deeper. A block bootstrap puts the difference in Sharpe ratio between that row and Whale One between −0.41 and +0.38, so the data cannot tell the two apart. Whale One is the choice of the shallower fall, made after seeing both.

The longer record#

The TSLA band cannot be tested before Nov 2018. The rest of Whale One can: TSM overnight with the gate, AAPL by day, and the R1 overlays, from Sep 2006.

The deepest fall ran from Oct 29, 2007 to Nov 20, 2008, −65.1%, and the line was not back at its peak until Jan 18, 2011. In 2008 it lost 54.2%, with the gate on for most of the year: the AAPL day leg lost money in those years, as the R1 page describes, and the gate does nothing for it. In the decade before the rule was found, these rules made 1.4% a year against QQQ's 12.4%.

What makes it fragile#

  • Eight years. The headline numbers cover Nov 2018 to Nov 2026 and nothing else. The twenty-year record of everything except the band is above, and its first decade is poor.
  • Hindsight, three times. R1 chose NVDA knowing how the decade went. Whale One chose TSM over NVDA, the gate, and TSLA for the band, each after seeing the result. TSLA was the only one of the names tested whose band passed.
  • The band is fading. At 50% of NAV it added 33.9% in 2020, between 7.9% and 12.8% a year from 2021 to 2023, and 2.3%, 5.7% and 5.5% in 2024, 2025 and 2026 to date.
  • Intraday exposure. The day book was at 150% of NAV on 660 of 2,012 days and at 200% on 13, when the TSLA band and the FTNT overlay were on together. It is flat by the close, and nothing is financed overnight.
  • One stock overnight. Up to 100% of NAV sits in TSM on ungated nights. TSM is the ADR of a Taiwan-listed company, so the overnight hold covers the Taipei session, and its tail is not the same as NVDA's: in the fall of Jan to Apr 2025 Whale One lost 16.7%.
  • The gate is one line. It worked in 2022. In 2008 the book still lost more than half.
  • Costs. At 3 basis points per side the return falls from +106.2% to +89.3% a year (Sharpe 2.18), at 5 to +71.9% (1.88). Filling the band leg one bar late at 3 basis points gives +102.3% (2.39).
  • No kill switches yet. R1 has six. Whale One has none, and it should not trade without them.

Status and current readings#

Whale One is on paper. Nothing trades on it.

  • Night gate: QQQ closed at 679.35 on Nov 9 against a 200-day average of 681.17, so the gate is on: under the rule no TSM is bought at the Nov 10 close.
  • Core: 80% of NAV, with the XOM gate open and no USO event running.

Status of these rulesWhale One is a paper rule set defined on Nov 10, 2026. It is not registered and no order is placed under it; the registered rule set is R1. Before it could trade it would need its own kill switches and the same 30-day cooling period as any other rule change.
