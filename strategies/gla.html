The rule in two chassis#

Most high-return US equity portfolios concentrate in correlated semiconductor and mega-cap software names whose drawdowns synchronize whenever real yields spike or the Nasdaq-100 breaks trend. GLA pairs two orthogonal secular compounders—Alphabet (GOOGL) in search/cloud AI and Eli Lilly (LLY) in biopharma (20-year daily return correlation 0.26)—with Apple (AAPL) and the rare Fortinet (FTNT) non-earnings opening gap-down reversal, while parking gated cash strictly in 0–3 month US Treasury bills (SGOV or BIL).

Two variations serve different account structures without carrying overnight margin:

  1. GLA-Overlay (B3 + FTNT, slot-swapped <= 200% intraday cap): Holds an unleveraged 100% all-day core (50% GOOGL + 50% LLY), steps GOOGL into SGOV whenever QQQ closes below its 200-day simple moving average, steps LLY into SGOV only if LLY closes below its own 200-day average on a monthly first-trading-day check, and runs 100% AAPL from the opening auction to the closing auction (MOO → MOC, zero overnight financing) while rolling 252-session AAPL intraday return (F-3) exceeds zero. On the roughly seven to eight non-earnings sessions a year when FTNT indicates at least 2% below its prior close at 09:28 ET (prior VIX < 30), half of the daytime AAPL slot swaps into FTNT (50% AAPL + 50% FTNT MOO → MOC), keeping peak intraday gross exposure at or below 200% and overnight exposure at 100%.
  2. GLA-Core (A5 + FTNT split gate, or A6 + FTNT dual gate): Holds an unleveraged 100% spot portfolio (1/3 GOOGL + 1/3 LLY + 1/3 AAPL, rebalanced only on ±20% relative weight drift or gate transitions). When QQQ closes below its 200-day average, the two tech sleeves (GOOGL and AAPL, 66.7% of NAV) step into SGOV while LLY (33.3%) stays invested (A5), or checks its own monthly 200-day average (A6). On FTNT <= −2% non-earnings trigger mornings, +50% FTNT runs intraday (MOO → MOC), lifting exposure to 150% on roughly 3% of sessions and returning to 100% by 16:00 ET.

Why the QQQ 200-day gate is split — and why TLT is excluded#

Applying a single QQQ < 200-day switch to the entire portfolio looks intuitive, yet twenty-two years of daily data expose a sharp divergence across the three holdings. Over the ten-year window (Nov 24, 2016 → Nov 24, 2026, 2,513 trading days), QQQ spent 424 sessions (16.9% of the decade) below its 200-day average:

  • GOOGL lost −11.1% cumulative (−6.8% annualized) and AAPL lost −12.8% cumulative (−7.8% annualized) across those 424 below-trend sessions; over the twenty-year record including 2008, ungated GLA-Core suffered a −54.6% drawdown (−83.7% for the ungated daytime overlay B0).
  • LLY gained +76.5% cumulative (+40.2% annualized) during the exact same 424 sessions while QQQ sat below its 200-day line—including +34.2% in 2022 when QQQ fell −32.6%. Forcing LLY to cash whenever QQQ breaks trend (A1) turns 2022 from +8.0% (A5 + FTNT) into a loss (−12.7% without FTNT).

Blanket-gating all three names when QQQ breaks its 200-day line discards Eli Lilly's counter-cyclical gain (+40.2% annualized across QQQ bear sessions). Parking gated tech sleeves in 20-year Treasuries (TLT, -31.2% in 2022) imports rate-hike duration losses just when the QQQ gate turns defensive; 0–3 month T-bills (SGOV / BIL) carry near-zero duration and earned +14.8% in 2022.

Equally critical is what replaces gated equity. Substituting 20-year Treasury bonds (TLT) for 0–3 month Treasury bills (SGOV / BIL) fails during inflation-driven rate shocks—the exact regime (2022) when the QQQ 200-day gate stays defensive for ten months:

What makes it fragile#

  • Three-name selection hindsight. GOOGL, LLY, and AAPL were selected knowing their decade-long dominance in search, GLP-1 therapeutics, and consumer hardware. If LLY enters a patent cliff or clinical setback while QQQ is simultaneously below its 200-day line, A5 takes a full one-third single-stock hit (which is why B3 and A6 retain LLY's monthly 200-day trend filter).
  • The daytime AAPL regime flip (2006–2015). Between 2006 and 2015, AAPL earned virtually all of its return overnight and lost −9% to −10% a year from open to close. Without kill switch F-3, B2 drew down −74.8% across 2008. Gating the AAPL daytime overlay on trailing 252-day F-3 > 0 (B3 + FTNT) cut 2008 losses to −9.7%, yet B3 + FTNT still trailed A5 + FTNT during the 2006–2016 decade (+7.1% vs +19.1% annualized).
  • Auction slippage on the daytime overlay. Because GLA-Core (A5 + FTNT) trades fewer than ten times a year, raising per-side costs from 1.2 bp to 10 bp barely dents its ten-year annualized return (+39.07% → +38.80%). By contrast, B3 + FTNT executes 500 AAPL auction orders a year: at 3 bp per side its ten-year return eases from +54.23% to +50.1%, and at 5 bp to +45.6%. Only exchange opening and closing auctions (MOO / MOC) are permitted.
  • Intraday buying-power ceiling. B3 + FTNT runs at 200% gross exposure between 09:30 ET and 16:00 ET (100% core + 100% daytime AAPL or 50% AAPL + 50% FTNT). Accounts without 2x Reg-T / portfolio intraday buying power should run GLA-Core (A5 + FTNT), which stays at 100% on 97% of sessions and peaks at 150% only on the ~7.5 FTNT sessions a year.
