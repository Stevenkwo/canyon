Barbell: Shaanxi Coal and the Nasdaq-100

A barbell: a coal stock on one end, the Nasdaq-100 on the other. The two tend to peak at different times. One rule in force nudges the coal position inside a ±10% band. On paper, a three-zone version sets the pair at 90/10, 60/40 or 30/70 by the rank, and two more legs sit on top: a margin add to the Nasdaq-100 when VIX closes above 30, and a one-day buy when the ETF gaps down 3%.

The signal#

The two ends of the barbell are Shaanxi Coal (601225) and a Nasdaq-100 ETF. Their daily moves are close to uncorrelated, which is what makes the pair worth rebalancing at all. The signal that sets the weights is called CF: cuòfēng, Chinese for "staggered peaks". The off-peak spread compares the last 42 trading days of Shaanxi Coal (601225, total return) with the Nasdaq-100 over the same days:

spread = coal's 42-day return − β × QQQ's 42-day return, with β estimated over the last 90 days

The rank is the spread's percentile within its own trailing two years (504 trading days). A high rank means coal has run well ahead of anything the Nasdaq-100 explains. A low rank means it has lagged.

In practice β sits close to zero (median +0.03), so the rank is almost exactly coal's own 42-day momentum rank. The correlation between the two is 0.989, and they place the day in the same third of the range 95.7% of the time. The rank is a mean-reversion gauge for an A-share stock, corrected slightly when the Nasdaq-100 moves hard.

Band rule, in force since Jul 18, 2026#

The coal position has an anchor size. Its target moves with the rank:

Rank Target
10 or below 110% of the anchor
90 or above 90% of the anchor
In between 100% of the anchor

The position never leaves the 90–110% band, and the Nasdaq side never trades on this signal.

Evidence at the low end. Over ten years the first day of each low-rank episode (rank 10 or below, 19 episodes) was followed by +5.2% over 42 days, with 71% up. All days together gave +3.7% and 63% up. The advantage belongs to the first day of an episode. Measured over every low-rank day instead, it disappears: +4.3% over 42 days, against +4.4% for all days in a sample extended to October.

Evidence at the high end. The top of the range is risk control rather than return. The chance of a drawdown of 5% or more within 20 trading days changes with the rank:

Rank Chance of a 5% drawdown within 20 days
Below 50 27–37%
70–80 56%
90–100 68%

Every 10 points of rank deepened the typical 20-day drawdown by 0.4 percentage points (Newey-West t = −3.6), in both halves of the sample. The direction of the next 42 days is less reliable: the rank predicts it in the first half of the sample, and the effect disappears in the second.

Swap rule, research on paper#

The swap rule treats coal and the Nasdaq-100 as a 50/50 sleeve. A three-zone version replaced it on Oct 29 (below); the swap is kept here as the first form of the idea.

  • Enter: when the rank reaches 85, move 20 points from coal to the Nasdaq-100 (30/70) at the next close.
  • Exit: when the rank falls below 50, go back to 50/50 at the next close.
  • Re-arm: the rank has to fall below 50 before the swap can trigger again.
  • In between: the sleeve rebalances only when a weight drifts more than 20% from its target.
  • Costs: 0.05% per side.

Measuring the swap fairly#

The swap trades far more often than a banded 50/50 sleeve: 36 to 48 rebalances in ten years, against 3 or 4. Extra rebalancing between two volatile, weakly correlated assets earns about 0.7–0.9 points a year by itself. So the benchmark is a placebo: a 50/50 sleeve rebalanced on exactly the same days, without changing the weights. Only the excess over the placebo is credited to the tilt.

Rank exits between 70 and 50 form a plateau. Day counts do not: 45 days looks good only because 40 and 50 do not.

Exiting on the rank works across a range. Exits at 70, 60, 50, 40 or 30 all add +2.3 to +2.8 points a year over ten years and +2.6 to +4.3 over six, positive in both halves.

Exiting after a fixed number of days does not.

  • Holds of 5 to 30 days add almost nothing.
  • 45 days is an isolated peak between weaker 40- and 50-day results.
  • A 60-day cap on top of the rank exit costs nothing (+3.0 points over ten years), while caps of 45 days or less cut the result.

The gain arrives late in each swap. The median swap lasts 27 trading days. The waiting period nets out close to zero, and the last two weeks before the exit carry the result. Part of that is by construction, because the rank only falls once coal has fallen.

Three zones instead of a swap#

The Oct 29 version drops the 50/50 base and lets the rank set the pair directly. Coal takes 90% of the sleeve when the rank is 10 or below, 60% in between, and 30% at 90 or above. A zone change at one close is executed at the next close, at 0.05% a side. Inside a zone nothing trades. The placebo rebalances to a fixed 60/40 on the same days.

The excess over the placebo is +2.1 points a year, from 107 zone changes. It is not evenly earned: +12.1 points in 2021 and +8.2 in 2026, both coal years, against −9.9 in 2022. Take those two coal years out and the tilt loses 1.3 points a year. The 60% midpoint is not a tuned number; moving it 30 points either way changes the excess by less than 0.1.

The three-zone sleeve carries no debt. Two overlay legs add some, each for a fixed period, each from a pre-written trigger.

Leg 1, VIX above 30. When the VIX closes above 30, the sleeve borrows 12.5% of its value at the next A-share close and buys the Nasdaq-100 ETF. The position is held 126 trading days, then sold and the loan repaid. One position at a time; interest at 6% a year.

Leg 2, the 3% gap. When the ETF opens 3% or more below the previous close, the sleeve borrows 20% of its value, buys at the open and sells at the next day's close. Round trip 0.08%.

Debt is capped at 40% of the sleeve. With both legs on at once it peaks at 34%; a lower cap of 30% would have blocked 18 of the 27 gap trades, because leg 1 was already borrowing on those days.

One position at a time, so a second close above 30 during a hold does not add. The Dec 2021 entry is the one loss in both windows.

Leg 1 is the larger of the two and the more repeatable. It fired six times in six years and nine times over ten years with QQQ as the leg. Over ten years it adds +1.8 points a year; eight of the nine episodes were up, and the one loss was the Dec 2021 entry, −29% on the position. The sleeve carried the loan 52% of the time and paid 4.9% of its value in interest over the six years. At 8% interest the six-year gain is +2.2 points instead of +2.3. Entering at VIX 25 instead of 30 earns less, +1.9.

Leg 2 lives on three days. Twenty-seven trades, 78% up, +1.3% each on average. Size is a volume knob, not a quality knob: every 5 points of size adds about 0.35 points a year and leaves the Sharpe ratio where it was. At 20% the worst trade (Aug 2, 2024, −9.8%) costs the sleeve 2.0% in a day and the best (Apr 9, 2025, +13.9%) adds 2.8%. The 2024 trades cost the sleeve 2.0% in total; the 2025 trades made 5.9%, three April sessions most of it. Without those three sessions the leg's six-year contribution falls from 7.0% of the sleeve to 2.5%. Sold at the same close instead of the next, the leg earns nothing (+0.1 points). The factor pages give the base rates: the same-day trade has no edge in any window, and the next-close trade keeps its edge only in the crash clusters.

It tracks the same index with its own premium, which has run higher than 513300's. Why the swap is not in force#

The parameters were chosen in sample. The 85 entry was picked while watching the current episode, and the exit below 50 was chosen during the research. The statistical support is marginal. For an earlier version of the signal that moved a smaller, fixed amount:

  • a circular-shift permutation test gave p ≈ 0.10 with QQQ and p ≈ 0.04 with the ETF;
  • by episode, the swap won 61% and 65% of the time (t = 1.9 and 2.3).

It conflicts with three standing rules. The three-zone version conflicts with the same three, and its 90/10 end would also have to sell the frozen ETF.

  • A 30/70 tilt pushes the coal position far outside its 90–110% band.
  • The band rule says the Nasdaq side never trades on this signal.
  • The account's own Nasdaq-100 ETF holding is frozen by rule.

A live version would need a new rule proposal and the 30-day cooling period. It would also need a different Nasdaq-100 ETF and the premium discipline for China-listed foreign ETFs. It should be planned at half the backtest edge.

The two overlay legs borrow. The draft caps the sleeve's debt at 40% and reached 34% with both legs on; the account's structure caps leverage at 1.2× and targets none. Both legs would need their own proposal, and leg 2 would need a falsification clause written before the first trade: it earns its history in a handful of crash sessions and lost money in every 2024 trade.

Two versions of the rank disagree at the margin. The official script works on additively adjusted prices. Until about mid-December it also uses a shorter window (466 days instead of 504), because of a gap in its QQQ history. In the current episode:

  • Entry: the official rank peaked at 84.5 on Oct 12, never reaching 85, while the research version (total-return prices) reached 86.9.
  • Exit: the two split the same way on Oct 23, at 49.6 against 50.4.

A live rule would first need one definition.
