Day and Night

NVDA by night, AAPL by day. Two auction orders on each side of the session: buy NVDA at the close and hold it overnight, swap it for AAPL at the open, sell AAPL at the close. Two gated energy legs and an FTNT gap-down overlay sit beside it.

The rule in one paragraph#

At the closing auction the account buys NVDA and holds it overnight. At the next opening auction it sells NVDA and buys AAPL. At the closing auction it sells AAPL and buys NVDA again. The core never holds both names at once. Three smaller legs sit beside it: XOM, 20% of NAV while XOM is above its 200-day average (checked once a month); USO, 20% of NAV for 21 trading days after the oil-volatility index OVX closes back below 50; and FTNT, an extra 50% of NAV bought at the open and sold at the close on days it opens at least 2% below the prior close. Nothing is financed overnight, and no order depends on judgment during the session.

The backtest charges 1.2 basis points per leg for a round trip, the fee level of auction orders. Slippage beyond that comes straight out of the result. In the registered study (data to Sep 21, 2026), raising the cost to 3 bp per leg cut the ten-year return from 85.0% to 71.5% a year, and 8 bp cut it to 38.8%. That is why only auction orders are allowed.

Why the day is split this way#

The rotation holds each stock for the half of the day in which it earned its return over the registered study's ten years. In those years the mirror image, AAPL overnight and NVDA by day, made +3.6% a year, and the pattern held in both halves of the decade. The decade before shows the same split for NVDA, much weaker, and the opposite split for AAPL:

From 2006 to 2016 AAPL made its whole return overnight and lost money in the session, so the day leg held the wrong half of it. Which half of the day pays has changed from one decade to the next, and the only part of the rule that would notice is kill switch F-3.

The FTNT overlay fired on 77 days in the registered study's ten years, about eight a year. On those days FTNT rose from open to close 71% of the time, by an average of 122 basis points (t = 4.1), and the worst day was −4.1%. Without the earnings filter the hit rate drops to 64%: a gap-down on the day after earnings behaves like any other day. In the seven years from FTNT's listing in Nov 2009 to Sep 2016 it fired 38 times: 55% up, +76 basis points on average, t = 1.7.

XOM and USO are energy exposure with rules attached. The XOM gate helped more when checked monthly than daily or not at all. The USO leg is a probe. Its events are positive on average but not significant, so it runs under its own kill switch.

What makes it fragile#

  • The decade before. From Sep 2006 to Sep 2016 the same rules made +1.7% a year against QQQ's +12.4%, with a −75.7% drawdown. The night leg still earned +9.6% a year; the AAPL day leg lost 10.1% a year, because AAPL's return came overnight in those years. Money put in at the Dec 2007 peak stayed below that level until Nov 18, 2016.
  • Hindsight. NVDA was the strongest large-cap stock of the registered decade, and the night leg was chosen knowing that. With QQQ in its place the rotation returns about +37% a year over that decade. Kill switch F-1 exists for this reason.
  • One stock overnight. Up to 100% of NAV sits in NVDA every night. The night leg lost 41.1% in 2008 and 37.8% in 2022. The worst day, −22.2% on Jul 3, 2008, was the open after NVDA cut its revenue forecast, when it opened 28% lower; the worst month was Sep 2008, −31.7%.
  • Costs. Every basis point of slippage per leg costs several points of annual return; see the cost test above.
  • Intraday exposure. On FTNT trigger days the day book reaches 150% of NAV through intraday buying power, and it is flat by the close.
  • The oil probe can misfire. OVX fell through 50 on Mar 2, 2020, just before the oil crash. USO lost 57.6% over the next 21 days, about −11.5% of NAV at a 20% weight.

Kill switches and current readings#

The kill switches are armed from the day the rule takes effect, and each one acts mechanically.

F-5 First 20 days: median auction slippage above 5 bp Review execution Collecting: 7 auction fills in week 1
F-6 Last 20 FTNT triggers: under 50% up or a net loss; or one trigger loses 8% Close the FTNT overlay 65% up, +17.4%, last trigger Oct 21: clear

Had the switches been armed in Sep 2006, F-3 would have fired on Sep 24, 2007, the first day the AAPL day leg had a full 252-day record, because it was already below zero. F-2 would have stopped R1 on Feb 15, 2008, at −40% from the Dec 2007 peak, on the way to −75.7%. F-1 would have fired on Jul 3, 2008. The backtest above runs without the switches, so it shows what they are there to prevent.

Gate status at the Oct 30 close:

  • XOM: 187.25 against a 200-day average of 153.39, gate open. The monthly check falls on Nov 2, the first trading day of November.
  • USO: the Oct 5 event reaches its 21st trading day on Nov 3 and is sold at that close. OVX closed at 58.5, so the leg is re-armed: a close below 50 would start a new 21-day event.
  • Core: 60% of NAV until the USO event ends, 80% from the Nov 3 close while the XOM gate stays open.
  • FTNT overlay: VIX at 19.4, so the overlay is live. FTNT reported on Oct 28; no earnings exclusion applies in the week of Nov 2.

Execution card · week of Nov 2#

From Nov 2 the United States is on standard time: the open is 09:30 ET = 22:30 Shanghai, the close 16:00 ET = 05:00 Shanghai the next morning. The card below is the whole week's instruction set; every order is an auction order placed before the exchange cut-off (opening auction 09:28 ET, closing auction 15:50 ET). Positions outside R1 are closed at Monday's opening auction. Sizes are recomputed each day from the actual NAV and the last price; the weights are the rule.

FTNT trigger days add 50% of NAV intraday (open to close). Intraday exposure therefore peaks at AAPL 80% + XOM 20% + FTNT 50% = 150% of NAV; overnight exposure never exceeds 100%, and there is no overnight margin.

Defined in advance, so that nothing is decided during the session:

  • A partial or missed auction fill is left alone; the next scheduled auction is the next order.
  • If XOM closes below its average on Nov 2 (a fall of about 17%), no XOM is bought and the core becomes 80% that night and 100% from Nov 3.
  • If the prior VIX close is 30 or higher, the FTNT overlay is off for that day.
  • An OVX close below 50 buys USO at 20% of NAV at that close for 21 trading days and lowers the core by 20 points.
  • Earnings, macro releases and large moves in NVDA or AAPL change nothing; the rule has no timing in it.

What the next review will score: fills at the auctions only, zero fills outside the rule, intraday peak under 3× NAV every day, NVDA at or below 1.0× NAV at every close, no pre-market or after-hours fills, and the hand (account minus the rule on paper) close to zero.

Amendments in the queue#

These amendments are registered and cooling. Until their dates R1 runs as described above.

v1.2 night gate (rule change #20, cooling to Nov 16). If QQQ closed below its 200-day average the day before, the account skips the NVDA purchase at the close and holds cash overnight. The day leg is unchanged.

  • Nights skipped: 965 in twenty years, 413 of them since Sep 2016. On those 413 nights NVDA averaged −13.3 bp with 47% up, against +24.4 bp and 60% up on the others.
  • Twenty years: +43.4% a year, Sharpe 1.35, maximum drawdown −48.1% instead of −75.7%; 2008 goes from −68.7% to −37.1%. The gate's own deepest fall comes later, Aug 2012 → Jan 2016, while QQQ was mostly above its average and the AAPL day leg was losing.
  • Since Sep 2016: +93.4% a year, Sharpe 2.36, maximum drawdown −33.5%; 2022 goes from −18.5% to +23.2%.
  • Years that get worse: ten of twenty-one, among them 2011 (−14.6 points), 2016 (−31.5) and 2020 (−13.2). The gate is slow when a recovery starts below the average. Its gains are concentrated in 2008 (+31.6 points), 2009 (+51.4) and 2022 (+41.7).

S-1 satellite (rule change #19, cooling to Nov 15). Buy NVDA with up to 10% of NAV, without new margin, when it closes 15% below its one-year high while QQQ is above its 200-day average. Exit after 21 trading days, when QQQ closes below its average, or when NVDA reaches −20% from the high.

  • History since 2014: 15 events, 80% up after 21 days, average +8.4%.
  • Against any bull-market day: 68% up and +5.6%. The difference is not significant (p ≈ 0.24).

What R1 does not do#

R1 does not use SQQQ or any other short of the index. It does not time the night or day leg on volatility. It does not hold FTNT every day or finance the energy legs. It does not trade before the open or after the close, and it uses no limit orders and no judgment during the session. Each of these was backtested and rejected.
