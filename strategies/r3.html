Half the NVDA through the day (draft)

A draft variant of R1. Keep half the NVDA through the session, double AAPL on intraday margin, and add a half slot for FTNT, TSLA or a BABA short. It is tracked on paper until Nov 26, 2026.

Verdict first#

The revised draft earns more than R1 in the backtest and takes more risk to do it.

R3 revised R1
Annual return +94.0% +85.9%
Sharpe ratio 1.77 2.05
Maximum drawdown −42.2% −39.7%
Worst day −14.7% −12.3%

In a block bootstrap (21-day blocks, 2,000 resamples), R3's Sharpe ratio beat R1's in only 2.15% of samples. Most of the gap comes from keeping NVDA in the day session and financing AAPL, not from the slot. By default the draft is rejected on Nov 26 unless it meets the conditions at the end of this page.

What changes from R1#

Overnight nothing changes: NVDA from the close to the open, the XOM and USO legs exactly as in R1, and no overnight financing. In the day session, every order is placed at the opening auction and reversed at the closing auction:

  1. NVDA. Sell only half the NVDA at the open and keep the other half (0.5 × core) until the close, when the half that was sold is bought back.
  2. AAPL. Buy AAPL at 1.0 × core on intraday margin.
  3. The half slot. The slot is 0.5 × core, also on intraday margin. It takes the first condition that holds that morning:
    • FTNT: a pre-market indication at least 2% below the prior close, with the same filters as R1's overlay.
    • TSLA: an opening at least 2% above the prior close.
    • BABA short: BABA's prior close is below its 200-day average. Short at the open, cover at the close.
    • Cash: if none of these holds, the slot stays empty.

Where the extra risk comes from#

Two related bets in the day session. Over ten years NVDA's session return has a Sharpe ratio of about 0.4, and its correlation with AAPL's session return is 0.55. Keeping half the NVDA while financing a full AAPL position stacks two related bets. Volatility rises from 33% to 43%.

Only FTNT has an edge. The other slot legs return about nothing. These are session returns on the days each condition held:

Slot leg Days Up Mean t Worst day
FTNT gap-down, −2% or more 77 71% +122 bp 4.1 −4.1%
TSLA gap-up, +2% or more 281 49% +5 bp 0.2 −9.9%
XOM gate open (first draft only) 1,609 51% +5 bp 1.4 −5.6%
BABA short, below its 200-day average 1,128 50% 0 bp 0.0 −14.1%

R1 already holds the FTNT edge as its overlay. In the first draft XOM took the slot on 55% of days for a near-zero return, and the drawdown deepened to −51.4%, against −46.9% with the slot limited to FTNT and TSLA.

The BABA short hedges; it does not earn. With the slot limited to FTNT and TSLA the drawdown is −46.9%. Adding the BABA short brings it to −42.2%, because the short paid in the years Chinese stocks fell hardest. It cost something in most other years, including 2026 to date.

Leverage every day. The R3 day book runs at 160–200% of NAV, which breaks Precept 4 (no leverage) on every trading day. R1 goes above 100% only on FTNT trigger days, and only to 150%.

Paper tracking and the Nov 26 decision#

R3 stays on paper through its 30-day cooling period, and no orders are placed for it. On Nov 26 it is rejected unless one of these holds:

  • after at least 20 trading days on paper, R3 beats R1 on at least 55% of days, and its worst day is no worse than 1.2 times R1's worst day; or
  • the slot gets new conditions to replace the TSLA and BABA legs, and they test at t above 2 in sample, with the same sign in both halves of the decade.

R1 with the same margin slot added on top, keeping its own NVDA and AAPL legs, comes closest to R1: Sharpe 1.95, maximum drawdown −41.7%, and it beats R1 in 11% of bootstrap samples. That is still not an improvement.
